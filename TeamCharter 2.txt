1. Team Name
Mighty Mouses (Group 28)
SS5250, AW2761, MA3403, ECNT20, TM2367  and AMAF24
2. Mission Statement
Our mission is to collaboratively design, build, and optimise a reliable, fast, and wellcontrolled autonomous mouse capable of completing the 20m 20kHzguided track in the shortest consistent time.
3. Goals & Objectives
* Complete a functional sensing and filtering system capable of detecting the 20kHz signal by Wednesday.
* Implement and test the full control loop by Thursday afternoon.
* Test on small and midsize tracks, then complete at least one full maintrack lap during race trials.
* Maintain daily progress tracking and resolve blockers within one work session.
* Produce full documentation and code by Friday.

5. Resources & Constraints
Resources
* Laboratory Equipment: Oscilloscopes, lab power supplies, signal generators, 20?kHz test track modules, midsize tracks, and the main track used for qualification and race testing.
* Hardware & Components: Arduino ESP32, DC motors, provided chassis, 220?µH pickup coils, prototyping stripboard, resistors, bread boards, capacitors, transistors, diodes, op-amps, switches, wiring, and batteries. 
* Technical Support: Access to demonstrators and academic staff during scheduled store and Q&A times for troubleshooting, advice, and hardware checks. 
* Shared Tools & Workspaces: Soldering stations, hand tools, testing tables, and collaborative workspace throughout the week.
Constraints
* Track Access: Midsize and main tracks require qualification, and availability is limited due to high team demand.
* Time Limitations: Project must be completed within a strict 5day schedule, including design, build, test, and iteration cycles. 
* Component Restrictions: Shared hardware means some parts cannot be replaced immediately or are unavailable; chassis must not be drilled or permanently altered. 
* Technical Limitations: Noise, sensor imbalance, battery charge, and motor efficiency introduce realworld constraints requiring careful design

6. Operating Norms
* Daily Standup: 9:15 each morning to assign tasks, identify blockers, and plan lab activities. 
* Communication: WhatsApp for rapid updates; Microsoft Teams for sharing code, diagrams, documentation, images, and logs. 
* Documentation: All test logs, wiring diagrams, and notes stored in a shared Teams folder. 
* Meeting Etiquette: Stay focused, respect speaking order, and keep discussions technical. 
* Work Coordination: Subsystem tasks completed independently, then integrated during joint testing sessions, use GANTT chart for efficient time control.






7. Decision-Making Process
* Technical Decisions: Majority vote after group discussions; Team Lead resolves ties. 
* Subsystem Decisions: The engineer responsible for the subsystem makes recommendations; team validates before integration. 
* Urgent Testing Decisions: Testing Lead + subsystem engineer make onthespot adjustments to avoid wasting time. 
* Final Signoff: Team Lead and Testing Lead approve fullsystem readiness before qualification or race trials.
8. Conflict Resolution
* begin with a private, calm discussion between team members to clarify viewpoints. 
* If unresolved, escalate to Team Lead mediation, applying the “pause, restate, respond” approach to slow down heated exchanges. 
* If issues persist, adjust workload or rotate tasks to reduce friction. 
* The team follows a culture of constructive criticism and togetherness, where ideas are critiqued, not people, and ideas are built upon.
9. Team Values & Culture
* Respectful Debate: All viewpoints are heard; disagreements stay technical.
* Accountability: Each member owns their subsystem and communicates progress clearly.
* Inclusivity: Everyone contributes to design, testing, and decisions.
* Iterative Engineering: Rapid prototyping and testing are prioritised over perfection.
* Controversy with Civility: Difficult discussions follow structure and logic to resolve scenario.
